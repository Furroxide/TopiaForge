# Changelog

## 0.1.0-rc.1

- Added the stable Unity-free multiplayer authoring contract and generated registration, codecs, snapshots, and prediction support.
- Predicted handlers can keep per-player state: they may read the arrays in their request and state, copy and resize arrays, and call the mod's own non-virtual methods, whose bodies the generator checks by the same rules. TFMP006 now says how to move other work out of a prediction. Work the generator cannot read stays refused: `this` handed to a helper or kept in a local, an event, an interface's or overridable property, a helper whose call runs a static constructor, `new T()`, an async helper, and a static `ReplicatedState<T>`.
- Added the solo provider, which runs the multiplayer session engine in single-player play while live transport remains unsupported.
- A `[NetworkBound]` the compiler cannot resolve no longer raises TFMP004 beside the compiler's own CS0246, and the multiplayer template's payload file has the `using` the attribute needs.
- Added the live transport's cryptography (X25519, Ed25519, ChaCha20-Poly1305, HKDF and the Noise XX handshake) over a vendored BouncyCastle subset, with its notices in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`. Nothing uses it until live transport ships.
- Added the live transport's secure datagram layer: the handshake over datagrams with stateless cookies and handshake budgets, encrypted packets with replay protection, loss recovery, a flow-controlled session stream, sequenced datagram channels and a paced outbound budget. Nothing uses it until live transport ships.
- Added path validation to the live transport, so a connection follows a peer whose address changed and upgrades from a relay to a direct path, plus a relay-only mode that never touches a direct path. Also added per-class shares for voice and realtime traffic, a shared host uplink that sheds voice first under pressure, and a bounded info query that lets players see a session before joining. Nothing uses them until live transport ships.
- Added the multiplayer identity (transport and signing keys in the provider's data folder), signed server descriptors in canonical JSON, `topiaforge://join` links and invite codes, LAN beacons and session info, password proofs and per-host player keys, with fixtures the launcher and the control plane replay. Nothing uses them until live transport ships.
- The session engine can now move a running process between solo play and another host's session, and a host can close its connections and play on, without restarting the session for mods: each change reaches them as a session replacement. Nothing uses it until live sessions ship.
- Added the voice codec: Opus at 48 kHz mono in 20 ms frames, with discontinuous transmission and in-band FEC, over a vendored Concentus subset with its notices in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, plus the Speex resampler that capture will use. Nothing uses it until voice chat ships.
- Added live LAN and direct sessions, off unless a developer switch turns them on: a network thread that keeps connections alive through main-thread stalls, the descriptor endpoint and LAN discovery, hosting with bans and an optional password, and joining that checks the game build, protocol and content before anything connects, then reconnects within the host's grace period. A relay-only player never contacts an address beyond the local network, and no address reaches mods or logs.
- Added the session engine's voice path: frames in pairs on the unreliable voice channel, stamped and relayed by the host under per-speaker rate, size and codec checks that mute and then remove abusers, server and listener mutes, and a per-speaker jitter buffer with FEC, concealment and drift control. Nothing uses it until voice chat ships.
- Hardened live sessions against a 30-minute soak of a host and four players on a lossy network and against seeded fuzzing of everything they read from other players. A host now lets go of a departed player's rate limiters, join content whose length is spelled past its bound is refused, and a client whose session ends partway through the host's snapshot keeps none of it in the solo session it returns to.
- Added the internal control TopiaForge's runtime drives multiplayer through: hosting, joining, queries, LAN listings, moderation with server mute, session replacement, the runtime's system channels and readiness holds, all behind opaque join targets that never show an address. Live sessions now take their content from the runtime: the host lists its shared packages, admits players through the runtime's admission planner, and a joining player checks the host's implicit packages exactly and its required session mods for presence before anything connects. The system channels pace every sender with a credit window that holds back only its own stream, restart with a new generation when a player rejoins, credit a newer peer's channels, and end a player that floods them or stops taking them; readiness holds are bounded however often the host replaces the session; a package that cannot be read stops hosting or joining with a failure of its own; and the host's own words never reach a log or a mod, while every log line is redacted, the provider's own included.
- Added NAT traversal for Internet sessions: a STUN client and a TURN relay client on the game's own socket, connectivity checks that start a session on the relay and move it to a direct path when one works, consent checks, relay credentials that rotate before they expire, a relay that is allocated again whenever it is lost, signaling through the control plane over a WebSocket that a joiner closes once connected, and best-effort UPnP and NAT-PMP port mapping while hosting. Relay-only is the default for the NAT layer and the transport alike, and never sends this machine's own addresses anywhere. Nothing uses it until Internet sessions ship.
- The provider can take the session the launcher asks it to host or join (launch request V5) and plan it onto live sessions: host settings, or a join target pinned by the server the launcher verified. Nothing starts it until the in-game flows ship.
- Added the voice service behind the live multiplayer switch: capture at any device rate, push-to-talk and voice activation with a 300 ms hangover, a voice worker thread that encodes and decodes, per-speaker jitter buffers, and wait-free playback streams for the audio thread. Voice stays off for each player until they turn it on. The voice codec now targets 20 kbit/s, so a talker fits the transport's voice share. Nothing uses it until voice chat ships.
- While a player joins another host, the session now lists nobody until that host's own list arrives behind its snapshot, rather than the previous host's players beside the new host's ids, which could mark the wrong player as the local one. A reconnect to a host that kept the player's record keeps the list, and a lost link never clears it, so voice streams carry on through an outage. A join that withholds the list ends every voice stream of the session before, even when the new host's whole answer arrives at once, so a stream never goes on to play whoever the new host gave that id.
- Added Internet sessions through the TopiaForge service: a bounded HTTPS client for the control plane that follows no proxy or redirect and reads every answer strictly; public and invite-only hosting that registers with the host's identity key, keeps its listing with heartbeats, registers again after a lapse, publishes newer descriptors, makes and withdraws invites, and deletes its listing when it stops; and joining by invite code or server link through a join ticket, relay credentials, signaling and the NAT layer. Relay-only is the default and is honoured whatever it costs: without the relay a join fails with a reason of its own and never falls back to a direct path. Every failure has its own code and a sentence in this game's own words, and no address, token, ticket or invite code reaches a log. The service's origin comes from the session launch or the manager's configuration; without one, nothing is contacted. Nothing starts them until the in-game flows ship.
- Hosting and joining now wait while TopiaForge identifies this game's packages off the main thread, so a large hand-copied package never holds a frame: hosting is refused as not ready yet, a join and the developer switch wait, and nothing is announced or admitted until the packages are identified.
- Live sessions can now be hosted, found, joined and left from inside the game, still behind the developer switch: TopiaForge's overlay has a MULTIPLAYER tab and a connecting overlay, with the approved privacy prompts. LAN and direct sessions need no service. With a TopiaForge service configured, Public and invite-only sessions, invite codes and server links go through it, on the relay unless the player turns Hide my IP off or allows a direct connection for that one join.
- The runtime's internal control now hands out the voice service: voice on and off, push-to-talk, listener mutes read back as the engine keeps them, playback streams that end for good rather than ever playing someone else after a join, a way to ask for a stream again that costs nothing, and counters mapped by what they mean. Server mutes are now the engine's, so a host's mute silences the player's voice at the relay as well as their chat, automatic mutes count as mutes, joined players can see them, and stopping hosting forgets them. Nothing uses it until voice chat ships.
- Hosting, joining and LAN browsing now work where the operating system has no IPv6, as on Linux with IPv6 turned off, the game under Proton included. Before, the game's sockets could not open there, so hosting failed as though the port were in use, browsing as though another program held the discovery port, and every join as though the host were unreachable. The game's socket, the descriptor endpoint and its client now fall back to IPv4 when the system refuses IPv6, and keep the dual-mode socket, unchanged, wherever IPv6 exists. Players and mods see no difference: a session over IPv4 behaves exactly as before, and nothing names an address.
- A gamemode's or a player's scope that resolves the multiplayer session no longer takes the whole mod with it when it stops. Closing a scope's session now retires only what that session made: the objects it spawned leave every player's game, and its commands still waiting for the host complete as cancelled. The mod's own objects and submissions stay until the mod itself stops.
- Added deterministic admission metadata, ownership validation, rollback/replay, and late-join state handling.
- Every command in a generated contract now has a `Submit…` method that hands the host's answer to a callback on the main thread: before the call returns where this process is the host, solo play included, and otherwise in the frame that brings the host's result. The answer runs once, for the player whose action submitted the command, as the code after an `await` does, never after the mod stops, its binding is released or the caller's cancellation token is cancelled, and a callback that throws is reported without disturbing the session. Nothing is polled, so a mod with nothing in flight pays nothing per frame. TF1008 now names this method when code waits on a command's task, and its IDE quick fix rewrites the wait into it.
- The in-game LAN browser now lists at most eight sessions from one address, as the launcher's does, so a host announcing many servers can no longer hide every other session on the network. A session that moves to another port on its own address keeps its place.
